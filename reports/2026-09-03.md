### Why it matters

GitNexus has mature language-provider, scope-resolution, optional grammar, resolver-fixture, fingerprint and conformance machinery, but a new contributor currently has to infer the shipping sequence by reading several implementations and test tables. A focused guide reduces onboarding error and, more importantly, teaches the repository’s safety posture: fail closed on ambiguous/external imports, prefer shared scope contracts over parallel language-specific DAGs, use adversarial decoys in resolver fixtures, and ensure optional grammars have a required CI lane so green-by-skip does not masquerade as validation.

### Expected impact

- Shorter path for contributors adding a language provider.
- Fewer integrations that pass local happy-path tests while missing cross-language gates.
- Better preservation of resolver precision through explicit negative/ambiguous cases.
- Clearer review order: shared-code changes first, provider registration/captures/resolution second, fixtures last.

## Learning

The main reusable lesson is that language-support quality is governed less by raw parser coverage than by resolution contracts and negative evidence. A robust provider needs deterministic detection, grammar availability proof, exact scope facts, fail-closed import resolution, adversarial decoys, and enrollment in repository-wide conformance/fingerprint gates. Those are the failure points most likely to create plausible-looking but wrong graph edges.
